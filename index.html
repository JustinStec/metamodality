Unit I: Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Week 1: Being and Non-Being February 4(~40 pp.)
How can we think or speak "what is not"? This problem drives subsequent responses from Aristotle through Heidegger. Kingsley's reading situates Parmenides within an initiatory tradition.
  • Parmenides, "On Nature" (complete fragments)
  • Peter Kingsley, In the Dark Places of Wisdom (Parts One and Two most essential)
Week 2: Potentiality and Actuality February 11(~45 pp.)
Aristotle's potentiality (dynamis) answers Parmenides by locating possibility within beings themselves. The sea-battle problem asks whether future contingents have determinate truth values.
  • Aristotle, Metaphysics Book Θ, chapters 1–3 and 6–8
  • Aristotle, De Interpretatione, chapter 9
  • Charlotte Witt, "The Priority of Actuality in Aristotle"
Week 3: Essence and Existence February 18(~40 pp.)
Separating essence from existence, Avicenna makes contingency fundamental to everything except the Necessary Existent. This reconfiguration of Aristotle shapes Leibniz's later work on possible worlds.
  • Avicenna, The Metaphysics of The Healing, Book I ch. 5–6, Book VIII ch. 1–3
  • Peter Adamson, "From the Necessary Existent to God"
Week 4: Emptiness and Dependent Arising February 25(~25 pp.)
Does "possibility" mean anything once inherent existence drops out? Nagarjuna's tetralemma refuses the logical options that Western frameworks take for granted.
  • Nagarjuna, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, chapters 1, 2, 15, 24, 25 (Garfield trans.; commentary optional)
  • Jay Garfield, "Dependent Arising and the Emptiness of Emptiness"
Unit I Workshop — February 26 or 27
Unit II: Rationalism, Transcendentalism, and the Imaginal
Week 5: Compossibility and Possible Worlds March 4(~35 pp.)
Compossibility constrains which possibilities can coexist within a single world. The Monadology raises the question of what individuates possible worlds.
  • Leibniz, Monadology
  • Émilie du Châtelet, Institutions de physique, chapters 1–2
  • Leibniz, "On the Ultimate Origination of Things"
Week 6: Real and Logical Possibility March 11(~45 pp.)
What must any possible experience conform to? Kant's Postulates distinguish logical possibility from real possibility.
  • Kant, Critique of Pure Reason: "Postulates of Empirical Thought"
  • Jessica Leech, "The Function of Modal Judgment and the Kantian Gap"
Week 7: The Imaginal World March 18 (Spring Break—informal)(~35 pp.)
Ibn Arabi's imaginal world (ʿālam al-mithāl) occupies a realm between sensible and intelligible, where possibilities take on form. His framework transforms Avicenna's essence/existence distinction and anticipates Leibniz's concern with why these possibilities rather than others become actual. The Adam chapter introduces the human being as comprehensive mirror of divine names; the Joseph chapter develops imagination as an ontological realm through dream interpretation.
  • Ibn Arabi, Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam (Bezels of Wisdom), "Adam" and "Joseph" chapters (Austin trans.)
Unit II Workshop — March 19 or 20
Unit III: Phenomenology, Pragmatism, and Nothingness
Week 8: Conditions of Possibility March 25(~45 pp.)
Can a condition of possibility also be a condition of impossibility? Derrida's introduction examines how ideal objects arise in history yet claim eternal validity.
  • Husserl, "The Origin of Geometry"
  • Derrida, Introduction to "The Origin of Geometry", selections
Week 9: Death and Natality April 1(~45 pp.)
Heidegger transforms the Aristotelian vocabulary so that possibility becomes constitutive of Dasein; death individualizes as the ownmost possibility. Arendt's natality offers a counter-emphasis.
  • Heidegger, Being and Time, Division II ch. 1 (§§46–53)
  • Arendt, The Human Condition, chapter 5 §§24–26
Week 10: Firstness and Continuity April 8(~50 pp.)
Firstness is pure qualitative possibility, prior to relation or resistance. Peirce's continuum allows possibility to grade into actuality.
  • Peirce, "A Guess at the Riddle"
  • "The Continuity of Life: On Peirce's Objective Idealism"
Week 11: Nothingness East and West April 15(~35 pp.)
Nishida's "place of absolute nothingness" (zettai mu no basho) holds particulars without being one itself. Lalla's Kashmiri Shaivite poetry pursues nothingness through a different idiom.
  • Nishida Kitarō, An Inquiry into the Good, Part I ch. 1–3
  • Lalla, Naked Song, selections
Unit III Workshop — April 17 or 18
Unit IV: Process, Contemplation, Emanation
Week 12: Eternal Objects and Ingression April 22(~45 pp.)
Eternal objects ingress into actual occasions as pure potentials. Whitehead's God functions as the primordial envisagement of possibility, a role that recalls Leibniz, and Peirce's categories find new form in this processual framework.
  • Whitehead, Science and the Modern World, chapter 11
  • Isabelle Stengers, Thinking with Whitehead, selections
Week 13: Attention and Awareness April 29(~45 pp.)
Thompson and Varela bridge first-person contemplative methods and third-person neuroscience. Weil's account of attention resonates with contemplative approaches.
  • Evan Thompson, Waking, Dreaming, Being, chapter 1
  • Simone Weil, "Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies"
  • Francisco Varela, "Neurophenomenology"
Week 14: Emanation and Superabundance May 6(~40 pp.)
Possibility flows from the One's inexhaustible superabundance. This emanationist framework shaped Avicenna and passed into German Idealism.
  • Plotinus, Enneads V.1
  • Anne Conway, Principles of the Most Ancient and Modern Philosophy, selections
Unit IV Workshop — May 7 or 8
Unit V: Mathematical and Formal Possibility
Week 15: Set-Theoretic Potentialism May 13(~60 pp.)
Ruth Barcan Marcus founded quantified modal logic. Hamkins's multiverse holds many distinct concepts of set, each instantiated in a corresponding universe.
  • Ruth Barcan Marcus, "Modalities and Intensional Languages"
  • Hamkins, "The Set-Theoretic Multiverse"
  • Hamkins and Linnebo, "The Modal Logic of Set-Theoretic Potentialism"
Week 16: Producing the Possible May 20(~50 pp.)
How do possibility spaces themselves get produced? Foster's work shows tradition constraining what becomes thinkable while furnishing materials for innovation, bringing the semester's philosophical questions into contact with computational and sociological methods. Borges's story explores the literary form of branching possibility.
  • Koch, Silvestro, and Foster, "The Evolutionary Dynamics of Cultural Change (Metal Music)"
  • Jorge Luis Borges, "The Garden of Forking Paths"
